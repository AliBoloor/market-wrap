## Macro thesis and regime

August delivered positive U.S. equity returns, powerful AI-linked earnings and lower implied volatility, but it did not resolve the market's central conflict: **nominal growth and profits were strong enough to support risk assets, while energy and inflation were firm enough to keep tighter policy in play**. SPY gained 2.68%, QQQ 4.18%, and IWM only 0.94%; VIX fell 6.69%. The narrow leadership and rising 10-year yield made this a constructive but fragile risk-on month.

The month's end captured that tension. The S&P 500, Dow and Nasdaq all rose in August, but fell on August 31 as renewed U.S.–Iran strikes lifted oil and Treasury yields. Markets priced more than a 65% probability of a September 25-basis-point Fed increase after Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole message emphasized inflation control. ([Reuters month-end](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/us-stock-index-futures-dip-as-middle-east-strikes-worsen-inflation-fears-4882344); [Federal Reserve speech](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/warsh20260828a.htm))

## U.S. economy: slower headline growth, firm private demand

Second-quarter real GDP grew **1.5% annualized**, down from 2.1% in the first quarter. Yet real final sales to private domestic purchasers grew 4.2%, real GDI rose 2.2%, and current-production corporate profits increased $400.9 billion. The gap between moderate GDP and stronger private demand argues against treating the headline slowdown as recession evidence. ([BEA GDP release](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-second-estimate-and-corporate-profits-2nd-quarter-2026))

The consumer picture was less comfortable. July real PCE was essentially flat, the saving rate was 3.0%, and headline PCE inflation reached 3.7% year over year, with core at 3.3%. July CPI was 3.4% year over year, although monthly headline CPI slowed to 0.1% and core to 0.2%. July payrolls fell 23,000 while unemployment held near 4.1%. Taken together: activity had not collapsed, but households had limited savings buffers and inflation remained above target. ([BEA income and outlays](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-july-2026); [BLS CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_08122026.htm); [BLS employment](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.htm))

## Rates, policy and financial conditions

The U.S. 10-year yield ended August at **4.758%**, about 1 basis point above July's final close in the saved Yahoo series, while TLT gained only 0.33%. The curve and the month-end oil shock signaled that long-duration assets still faced inflation and term-premium pressure. Warsh's speech made September data-dependent rather than predetermined, but it moved the burden of proof toward softer inflation.

The policy problem is two-sided. A hike could cool inflation and reinforce credibility but tighten into slowing payrolls; a hold could support activity but risk validating looser financial conditions. The September 15–16 FOMC meeting therefore depends heavily on payroll, wage and CPI details, not merely headline employment. ([Federal Reserve September calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/2026-september.htm))

## Earnings and valuation

Second-quarter earnings were exceptional at the aggregate level. FactSet's August 7 snapshot estimated **50.4%** blended S&P 500 earnings growth, with 86% of reporting companies beating EPS estimates. But the growth rate fell to 32.0% excluding Alphabet and Amazon, illustrating concentration and the influence of non-operating gains. Nvidia then reported $96.22 billion of quarterly revenue, more than double the prior year, sustaining the AI investment narrative. ([FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/sp-500-earnings-season-update-august-7-2026); [Associated Press on Nvidia](https://apnews.com/article/dc8d556e709b50915cca9217a60b1991))

The implication is constructive but not valuation-insensitive: profits are supporting the market, yet QQQ's 4.18% monthly gain and leadership concentration leave it particularly exposed if capital-spending expectations or long yields turn adverse.

## Global markets and economy

Europe's STOXX 600 gained **0.3%** in August, its fifth consecutive monthly advance, supported by earnings and AI enthusiasm even as oil and yields rose at month-end. Germany's second-quarter growth was revised higher and business morale reached a one-year high. China remained a source of slower-growth risk, though the yuan strengthened to its best level against the dollar since early 2023. ([Reuters Europe](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/european-stocks-ease-as-u-s-iran-strikes-fuel-oil-rally-ce7858dcdc8efe26); [Reuters global dashboard](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/oil-yields-slide-4875984))

The dominant global transmission channel was energy. The Strait of Hormuz conflict kept crude volatile and raised inflation risk across importing economies, while gold's roughly 10% August surge and Bitcoin's 25% gain suggested continuing demand for scarce or non-sovereign assets. A softer dollar during much of the month provided some relief internationally, but the Friday reversal showed how quickly relative-rate expectations could change.

### Conditional scenarios

- **Base:** growth remains positive, inflation eases only gradually, and the Fed keeps policy restrictive. Equities consolidate above their 50-day averages while leadership remains concentrated.
- **Upside:** payroll and CPI details cool without a demand break, lowering the two-year yield and broadening participation. Confirmation would be SPY above 779 and IWM reclaiming 300.
- **Downside:** oil stays near or above $90 and inflation breadth worsens, or weak labor data changes falling yields from valuation relief into a recession signal. SPY below 749 and QQQ below 686 would mark deterioration.
