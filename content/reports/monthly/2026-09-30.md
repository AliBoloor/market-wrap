## Macro thesis and regime

September was not a broad equity advance. It was a **technology-led market sitting on top of materially tighter financial conditions**. QQQ gained 3.21%, but SPY lost 0.58%, IWM fell 5.46%, and the equal-weight S&P 500 ETF RSP fell 5.18%. Only technology rose among the 11 sector ETFs in the saved scorecard. At the same time, the official two- and 10-year Treasury yields each rose 54 basis points, the dollar index proxy gained 2.03%, TLT lost 5.74%, and investment-grade and high-yield bond ETF prices declined.

The macro mix explains the split without reducing the month to a single cause. Real activity and consumption remained positive, corporate earnings were strong, and AI-linked investment supported large technology companies. Inflation remained above target, energy prices were volatile, and the Federal Reserve raised rates. The result was **positive earnings momentum but restrictive discount rates, narrow risk appetite, and weak participation outside large growth stocks**. September's market evidence supports that regime description; it does not prove that one policy, data release, or geopolitical event caused every asset move. ([Federal Reserve statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm); [AP month-end close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-oil-bonds-us-ai-chips-e61d3d7b947a390c7eee6c8758e3095d))

## U.S. economy: growth continued, labor cooled, inflation stayed uncomfortable

The annual national-accounts update put second-quarter real GDP growth at **2.2% annualized**, down from a revised 2.5% in the first quarter. Consumer spending, investment and exports contributed. That is evidence of continued expansion, not recession, but it is backward-looking and does not erase the tightening visible in September's market prices. ([BEA GDP](https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gross-domestic-product))

August household demand was firm. Nominal consumption rose **0.9%**, real consumption increased **0.6%**, and the saving rate was **4.1%**. Headline PCE inflation was 3.4% year over year and core PCE inflation 3.0%. August CPI told a similar but not identical story: headline prices rose 0.4% in the month and 3.4% over 12 months, while core CPI rose 0.3% and 2.4%. Gasoline accounted for more than one-third of the monthly headline CPI increase. The evidence is consistent with resilient spending and incomplete disinflation, not a clean return to price stability. ([BEA personal income and outlays](https://bea.gov/index.php/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-august-2026); [BLS CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.htm))

Labor data weakened by the time this catch-up edition was generated. September payrolls rose only **29,000**, unemployment held at 4.2%, wage growth slowed to 0.1% month over month and 3.0% year over year, and July-August payrolls were revised down by a combined 60,000. The release was published October 2 and is therefore a post-month-end update, not information available at the September 30 close. August JOLTS had already shown 7.1 million openings, 5.2 million hires and 3.1 million quits—cooler turnover without a collapse in labor demand. ([BLS September employment](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm); [BLS August JOLTS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.htm))

The production and investment picture was more resilient than household confidence. August core capital-goods orders—nondefense capital goods excluding aircraft—rose 1.6%, and shipments increased 0.6%. Industrial production was unchanged, with manufacturing output down 0.3%. August new-home sales rose 6.4% to a 684,000 annual rate, but the change carried a ±19.5% margin and therefore was not statistically conclusive; inventory was 8.5 months of supply. These data support continued business investment and uneven housing, not a synchronized acceleration. ([Census durable goods](https://www.census.gov/manufacturing/m3/adv/current/index.html); [Census new-home sales](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrs/current/))

## Rates, policy, liquidity and credit

The FOMC raised the federal-funds target range 25 basis points to **3.75%–4.00%** on September 16. Its projections showed a 4.1% median year-end policy rate, 2.3% 2026 real GDP growth, 4.1% unemployment, 3.7% headline PCE inflation and 3.4% core PCE inflation. Those are policymaker projections, not guarantees. ([Federal Reserve statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm); [Federal Reserve projections](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260916.htm))

The Treasury curve repriced almost in parallel over the month. The official two-year yield rose from **4.34% on August 31 to 4.88% on September 30**, while the 10-year rose from **4.75% to 5.29%**. The 2s10s slope therefore remained near +41 basis points even though both maturities increased sharply. The long-Treasury ETF fell 5.74%, LQD fell 3.79%, and HYG fell 3.26%. These ETF moves are price proxies, not spread measures, but they confirm that tighter rates reached duration and credit assets. ([Treasury August curve](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value_month=202608&type=daily_treasury_yield_curve); [Treasury September curve](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value_month=202609&type=daily_treasury_yield_curve))

Liquidity cannot be inferred from one series. The stronger dollar, weaker bond ETFs and higher yields indicate tighter market financial conditions, while contained VIX and positive QQQ returns show that equity funding stress was not broad. Fiscal and Treasury-supply concerns are plausible contributors to the long-end move, but attribution is not clean enough to assign a precise share. October's coupon auctions are therefore confirmation tests rather than assumed causes.

## Earnings expectations and valuation

Second-quarter earnings remained a powerful support. FactSet's August 7 update estimated 50.4% blended S&P 500 earnings growth, but only 32.0% excluding Alphabet and Amazon, highlighting both strength and concentration. By late September, Reuters reported the S&P 500 trading at just under 19 times expected earnings, its lowest forward multiple since 2023. The combination of a lower multiple and indexes near records suggests that upward earnings expectations, rather than multiple expansion, were doing more of the work. That is an inference from the cited estimates, not a claim that valuation risk disappeared. ([FactSet Q2 earnings update](https://insight.factset.com/sp-500-earnings-season-update-august-7-2026); [Reuters valuation report](https://www.fidelity.com/news/article/us-markets/202609231600RTRSNEWSCOMBINED_L6N45F140_1))

The risk is asymmetric concentration. Strong AI and semiconductor demand can sustain aggregate profit growth while leaving the index vulnerable if capital-spending expectations weaken or long yields rise further. Third-quarter reporting had not broadly begun by September 30, so estimates and guidance should not be treated as realized results. October's earnings season must be judged on revenue breadth, margins, cash conversion and 2027 capital-spending guidance—not headline EPS alone.

## October scenarios

- **Base — narrow resilience with volatile long rates:** growth stays positive, labor cools, and inflation remains above target. The 10-year trades roughly around 5.0%–5.4%; SPY holds the 749.60–762.74 support structure, QQQ remains above 714.93, and IWM stays near but above 276.05. Technology leadership persists, but breadth improves only slowly. A sustained 10-year move above 5.4% with SPY below 749.60 invalidates this case.
- **Upside — disinflation and strong auctions broaden participation:** CPI/PCE and wage data cool, oil retreats, and Treasury auctions clear without a large concession. The 10-year falls below 5.0%, SPY clears 775.14, QQQ clears 748.35, and IWM reclaims 293.08. Confirmation would include RSP outperforming SPY, HYG/LQD stabilization and at least six sectors advancing.
- **Downside — inflation or supply shock reaches earnings and credit:** hot inflation, weak auction demand or renewed energy disruption pushes the 10-year above 5.4%; SPY closes below 749.60 and IWM below 276.05. Confirmation would be VIX above 20, further HYG/LQD weakness, a stronger dollar and downward revisions to non-technology earnings. A retreat in the 10-year below 5.0% with broad sector participation would invalidate this path.

These are conditional paths, not probability estimates. Inflation breadth, labor revisions, oil, Treasury-auction demand, the 10-year yield, credit proxies, equal-weight performance and earnings guidance are the main signposts.

## Data quality and limitations

- The deterministic collector saved 30 market histories clipped to September 30 and recorded no retrieval or OHLC-integrity failures.
- Market returns are unadjusted price returns. Cash indexes, ETF proxies, Treasury par yields, continuous futures, the VIX and Bitcoin are distinct instruments.
- Yahoo daily timestamps can label a bar's session rather than the precise closing instant. Continuous futures and Bitcoin are not simultaneous with the U.S. equity close.
- HYG and LQD are ETF price proxies, not credit-spread series. Regional ETFs include currency and U.S.-hours effects.
- Macro releases have different reference periods and revision policies. The October 2 employment report is explicitly identified as post-month-end information; no October market prices enter the monthly tables or charts.
- Earnings estimates and forward valuation are expectations, not realized results, and aggregate growth remains concentrated.
