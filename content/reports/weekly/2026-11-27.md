## Week in review

The holiday-shortened week delivered a **broad but incomplete rebound from the prior week's liquidation**. The S&P 500 cash index gained **5.60%**, its best week since April 2025, while the Dow rose 5.46%, the Nasdaq Composite 6.68% and the Russell 2000 cash index 6.53%. VIX fell from 35.89 to **23.81**, every tracked sector gained, high-yield and investment-grade bond ETF proxies edged higher, and Friday finished with four of every five S&P 500 stocks advancing. ([Friday market context](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-tariffs-ai-profits-energy-c7ef10c1dbc5fa09668bd4fe0729c613))

The path was not orderly. Monday's 4.73% SPX surge followed Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO and reversed much of the prior selloff; technology then pulled the cap-weighted indexes lower Tuesday and Wednesday even while most S&P 500 constituents rose. Friday's 1.60% gain broadened the rebound again. The evidence supports relief from extreme positioning and renewed appetite for AI exposure, but not a settled view on AI profitability, financing or regulation. ([Monday](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-rates-technology-ai-anthropic-49ba1beba78fa4ed2bd5898029f79c91); [Tuesday](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-ai-earnings-oil-feec0e25b9994b1c86b16329cf770192); [Wednesday](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-ai-earnings-oil-10044b4fb55d3ff20fde9ba9e9acaab1))

Cross-asset confirmation was constructive but not a clean reflation signal. HYG rose 0.37%, LQD 0.30% and official two- and ten-year yields fell through Wednesday, yet TLT finished down 0.22% after Friday's reported 10-year yield rebounded toward 4.94%. The Dollar Index rose 0.22%, gold gained 4.36%, WTI fell 1.07% and Bitcoin added only 1.61%. Equities recovered risk appetite; the simultaneous dollar and gold gains and oil decline show that inflation and geopolitical hedging remained active.

## Global week

U.S.-traded regional ETF proxies also rebounded: emerging markets (EEM) gained **7.91%**, Japan (EWJ) **6.76%**, China (MCHI) **4.27%** and Europe (VGK) **3.99%**. These dollar-denominated ETFs include currency and U.S.-hours effects; they are not local cash-index returns.

Asia's timing amplified the swings. South Korea's Kospi rose 6.3% and Japan's Nikkei 225 gained 3.9% Tuesday as those markets caught up with Monday's U.S. rally; both then fell Wednesday before global shares recovered while U.S. markets were closed for Thanksgiving. The synchronized rebound supports improved global risk appetite, but the stronger dollar, rising gold and continuing Iran-related oil-supply risk prevent a single-cause interpretation.

## Macro, policy and earnings pulse

- **Growth stayed strong, inflation stayed uncomfortable.** Third-quarter U.S. real GDP growth was revised up to a 4.3% annual rate, the fastest in three years. October headline PCE inflation accelerated to 3.8% year over year, core PCE was 3.3%, and real consumption edged lower. Strong activity alongside persistent inflation increases the importance of Friday's jobs report and keeps the December Fed path sensitive to incoming data. ([BEA personal income and outlays](https://bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-october-2026))
- **The curve flattened.** Official two-year yields slipped 2 bp and ten-year yields 7 bp through Wednesday, narrowing the 2s10s spread by 5 bp. Friday's price action reversed part of the duration rally, so the weekly message is modest easing in long yields, not an unbroken safety bid.
- **Consumers and earnings remained unevenly resilient.** AP reported improved consumer confidence and stronger-than-expected results from Dollar Tree and Kohl's. It also cited S&P 500 earnings-per-share growth above 25% for a third consecutive quarter. Those observations support an earnings cushion, while softer real consumption and high inflation argue against extrapolating a uniformly strong household.
- **AI governance and valuation dominated index behavior.** Nvidia's CEO reversal drove Monday's surge, but the subsequent pullback showed that leadership news did not resolve profitability, regulation or funding concerns. Next week's Marvell, Snowflake and Salesforce reports will test demand across AI infrastructure, cloud data and enterprise software.
