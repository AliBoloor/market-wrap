## Week in review

The week ended with a **technology-led equity advance colliding with a global bond selloff**. The S&P 500 cash index gained **1.21%** and the Nasdaq Composite rose **2.06%**, but the Russell 2000 lost **0.80%**, the long-Treasury ETF TLT fell **2.38%**, and the official U.S. 10-year yield climbed **16 basis points to 5.17%**. Growth ETF VUG beat value ETF VTV by 2.67 percentage points, and only four of 11 sector ETFs in the saved scorecard advanced. This was risk appetite concentrated in AI-linked growth rather than a broad easing of financial conditions.

Friday captured the tension. Microsoft and other AI-linked shares lifted the S&P 500 0.51%, the Nasdaq 0.48%, and the Dow 0.93%. Yet the 10-year yield had touched 5.2297%, its highest since 2007, and Brent remained above $100 even after falling on reports of possible U.S.-Iran de-escalation. Reuters also reported 54 Nasdaq new highs against 175 new lows and relatively light exchange volume. The closing gain was real, but the underlying participation was uneven. ([Reuters U.S. close](https://www.lse.co.uk/news/us-stocks-wall-street-ends-higher-as-investors-buy-ai-stocks-microsoft-rallies-51b4hknw3h4d10k.html))

The cross-asset regime is therefore **risk-on at the index level, restrictive beneath the surface**. The dollar gained, investment-grade and high-yield bond ETF prices fell, the Treasury curve bear-steepened, and rate volatility surged. Falling WTI and a stable VIX kept the move from becoming disorderly, while stronger capital-goods orders supported the AI investment narrative. The evidence supports higher discount-rate pressure and narrow equity resilience; it does not support a broad liquidity rally.

## Global week

The saved U.S.-traded regional proxies advanced in Europe (**VGK +0.48%**), Japan (**EWJ +0.96%**) and emerging markets (**EEM +1.42%**), while China-focused MCHI fell **0.85%**. These are investable dollar-based ETF proxies with currency and U.S.-hours effects, not local cash-index returns.

Reuters reported the MSCI world index heading for its best week since early August and the STOXX 600 gaining for the week. That equity resilience coexisted with a much harsher rates signal: Japan's 10-year yield reached **3.121%**, its highest since 1996; Norway raised rates; Sweden signaled that tightening was likely; and five of the G10's major central banks had raised rates during September. ([Reuters global markets](https://www.lse.co.uk/news/global-markets-battered-bonds-draw-support-from-falling-oil-prices-9tf1rbya1qd3cgh.html))

Energy and geopolitics remained the transmission channel. Brent settled Friday at **$104.32**, up less than 1% for the week, while WTI settled at $92.41 and fell about 8%. The Brent-WTI divergence reflects different contract and supply exposures and should not be treated as inconsistent data. Reports of a phased U.S.-Iran path toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz pushed prices lower Friday, but Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia kept supply risk elevated. ([Reuters oil close](https://www.lse.co.uk/news/oil-prices-slide-about-2-as-us-iran-explore-path-out-of-war-fucra3kxxplgq92.html))

The U.S.-China summit produced positive rhetoric but no reported breakthrough on trade, AI, Taiwan or the Iran war. Global equities therefore priced hope for energy de-escalation and AI earnings while global bonds continued to price inflation, fiscal and term-premium risk.

## Macro and earnings pulse

- **Manufacturing investment was stronger than the headline.** August durable-goods orders were virtually unchanged at $338.6 billion, but orders excluding transportation rose 0.3% and nondefense capital-goods orders excluding aircraft rose **1.6%**. Shipments in that core capital-goods category increased 0.6%. The figures are nominal, advance estimates and subject to revision. ([Census durable goods](https://www.census.gov/manufacturing/m3/adv/current/index.html))
- **Households were much less confident.** Final September University of Michigan sentiment fell to **48.1** from 51.7 in August. One-year inflation expectations rose to 4.6% from 4.0%, while five-year expectations edged to 3.4%. These are survey expectations, not realized inflation. ([Reuters on Michigan sentiment](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/us-consumer-sentiment-eases-to-four-month-low-in-september-ce785adfde8dff20))
- **Housing demand improved, but the estimate is noisy.** August new-home sales rose 6.4% to a 684,000 annual rate, but the change carried a ±19.5% margin and therefore was not statistically conclusive. Inventory remained 483,000 units, or 8.5 months of supply. ([Census new-home sales](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrs/current/))
- **The external deficit widened.** The second-quarter current-account deficit increased by $33.4 billion to **$246.0 billion**, or 3.0% of current-dollar GDP. The net international investment position deteriorated to -$22.42 trillion. ([BEA international transactions](https://www.bea.gov/data/intl-trade-investment/international-transactions))
- **Rates repriced more aggressively than equities.** The official 10-year yield rose 16 basis points on the week, while Reuters said the ICE BofA MOVE index jumped about 30%, its largest weekly increase since April 2025. Equity gains did not erase the tightening in borrowing costs.
- **AI remained the earnings and capex exception.** Microsoft rose Friday after announcing new Copilot capabilities, Akamai advanced on an Anthropic cloud agreement, and the technology ETF led sectors. Those developments support a narrow AI spending thesis, not an economy-wide earnings conclusion. ([Reuters U.S. close](https://www.lse.co.uk/news/us-stocks-wall-street-ends-higher-as-investors-buy-ai-stocks-microsoft-rallies-51b4hknw3h4d10k.html))

The combined evidence is consistent with **firm business investment and weak household confidence under tightening financial conditions**. Durable-goods internals argue against a sudden capex contraction, but rising long yields, weak rate-sensitive sectors, a wider current-account deficit and elevated energy risk complicate the growth outlook.

## Data quality and limitations

- The deterministic collector saved 33 market histories with September 25 observations and no retrieval or OHLC-integrity failures.
- Weekly changes are unadjusted price returns. Cash indexes, ETF proxies, continuous futures, Treasury par yields and spot assets are identified separately.
- Yahoo daily timestamps differ by venue and can mark the start of a session rather than its closing instant. The report labels the relevant session date; continuous futures and Bitcoin are not simultaneous with the U.S. equity close.
- HYG and LQD are ETF price proxies, not credit-spread series. Regional ETFs include currency and U.S.-hours effects.
- WTI continuous futures and Brent futures are different instruments. Brent figures come from a separately cited Reuters settlement record.
- The options snapshot is through Thursday's close. VIX, SKEW, MOVE and futures term structure describe different risks and do not identify trade direction or dealer positioning.
- Survey and economic releases are preliminary or revisable where the source says so. Calendar and earnings dates can change.
