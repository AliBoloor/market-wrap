## Week in review

The week was a **broad risk-off break that turned the technology correction into a market-wide drawdown**. The S&P 500 cash index lost **8.61%**, the Dow fell **8.02%**, the Nasdaq Composite dropped **10.02%**, and the Russell 2000 declined **7.14%**. Every tracked sector ETF fell, the S&P 500 closed lower in all five sessions, and VIX nearly doubled from 19.30 to **38.24**. By Thursday, more than 90% of S&P 500 constituents were down; Friday's final bar then pushed SPX below its rising 200-day average. ([Monday](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-ai-nvidia-oil-us-35786b8f539ec95f16ae0500f71cfcc7); [Tuesday](https://apnews.com/article/4d5a35288288389d894e63e6bebe8240); [Wednesday](https://apnews.com/article/dad5715bc91b4aba23a567a40e22b1fc); [Thursday](https://apnews.com/article/2f178054aea79d282e5ccf8d62f26a28); [Friday context](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-anthropic-ai-chips-boom-c4c3f4878b12989347f8a930ed813dad))

Technology remained the epicenter, but concentration alone no longer explains the tape. XLK fell 12.42%, yet financials, industrials, energy and materials each lost roughly 7%, HYG weakened, and all four regional ETF proxies declined. Nvidia reported exceptional growth and an above-consensus outlook, but its initial gain reversed; Anthropic's record IPO also failed to stabilize the complex. The market response supports a repricing of AI valuation, financing and regulatory risk rather than evidence that current AI demand has collapsed.

Cross-asset confirmation was decisively defensive. TLT gained 2.61% as the 10-year yield fell to roughly 4.91% Friday afternoon, while HYG lost 0.77%, the Dollar Index rose 0.44%, gold fell 9.53%, WTI crude fell 5.33%, and Bitcoin dropped 14.73%. Treasury duration provided safety, but gold and crypto did not. That mix is better described as deleveraging and a flight to liquid sovereign duration than a simple inflation-relief rally.

## Global week

U.S.-traded regional ETF proxies all fell sharply: emerging markets (EEM) lost **11.59%**, Japan (EWJ) **9.08%**, China (MCHI) **7.16%**, and Europe (VGK) **7.13%**. These dollar-denominated proxies include currency and U.S.-hours effects and are not local cash-index returns.

The technology unwind propagated globally. AP reported that Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi each fell more than 5% on Friday and that European indexes also declined sharply. The continuing U.S.-Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz tension remained oil-gap risks even as Brent eased, while Russia-Ukraine diplomacy produced no clear breakthrough. Attribution is therefore layered: the synchronized equity decline is consistent with AI-duration and global risk repricing, but geopolitical and currency effects prevent a single-cause explanation. ([Friday global context](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-anthropic-ai-chips-boom-c4c3f4878b12989347f8a930ed813dad))

## Macro, policy and earnings pulse

- **Imported inflation stayed firm.** October import and export prices each rose 1.1%; nonfuel import prices rose 0.8%, and capital-goods import prices rose 1.5%, the largest monthly increase in that series. Missing October 2025 survey data limited some annual comparisons. This evidence kept the inflation problem alive even as risk-off flows pulled long yields down. ([BLS import/export prices](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ximpim.htm))
- **Production and housing gave a mixed growth signal.** Industrial production fell 0.4% in October; manufacturing rose 0.3%, but manufacturing excluding vehicles edged down 0.1%. Housing starts rose 9.9%, though the confidence interval included zero, while permits fell 7.4%. The releases do not support a clean recession or reacceleration conclusion. ([Federal Reserve industrial production](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/20261117/g17.pdf); [Census housing](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/current/))
- **The Fed minutes highlighted both inflation and financial-stability risk.** Two voters had preferred a 25-basis-point hike in October. Staff also flagged rapid AI-related debt growth, off-balance-sheet leases and circular financing as potential amplifiers if AI returns disappoint. The minutes predated this week's selloff, so they are context rather than an explanation of Friday's close. ([FOMC minutes](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20261028.htm))
- **AI demand remained strong even as the equity multiple broke.** Nvidia reported $115.8 billion of revenue, up 103% year over year, and guided to $130 billion plus or minus 3% next quarter. The stock's reversal after those results separates strong current fundamentals from investors' willingness to capitalize them at prior valuations. ([Nvidia results](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx))
- **Large-retailer evidence was more resilient than the market.** Walmart reported total revenue growth of 5.2%, adjusted constant-currency operating-income growth of 5.5%, and 17% global e-commerce growth. That supports continued household spending capacity, though it does not negate selectivity or high-rate pressure elsewhere. ([Walmart results](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/11/walmart-releases-q3-fy27-earnings))
