## Week in review

The holiday-shortened week delivered an **oil-and-rates tightening shock with a late relief bounce**. From the prior Friday close, the S&P 500 fell **0.80%**, the Nasdaq Composite lost **0.66%**, the Dow declined **1.57%**, and the Russell 2000 dropped **2.41%**. The saved continuous WTI futures series rose **9.31%** to $100.00 a barrel, the 10-year Treasury yield index climbed **19.1 basis points** to 4.975%, and long-Treasury ETF TLT fell **1.63%**. Friday's 0.9% S&P 500 rebound, lower oil and a VIX close of 15.84 eased acute stress, but they did not reverse the week's tighter financial conditions or weak participation. ([Associated Press Friday close](https://apnews.com/article/financial-markets-stocks-oil-iran-war-bonds-rates-8c3272812f5e9b9238c6a3301921c17a); [Associated Press weekly index record](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-67a463295d9ea178d7802ca4338a6eb5))

The sequence matters. Equities fell in each of the first four sessions as escalating U.S.–Iran hostilities restricted Middle East oil flows, WTI and Brent moved through $100, producer inflation surprised on the firm side, and global sovereign yields rose. Friday's CPI was close enough to expectations and Brent retreated 2.8% to $104.61, allowing stocks to recover much of the day's risk premium. Attribution is strongest for the shared oil, inflation and rate channels documented by official releases and contemporaneous reporting; closing prices cannot isolate how much of each index move came from geopolitics, data, policy expectations or company news. ([BLS Producer Price Index](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ppi.nr0.htm); [BLS Consumer Price Index](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm); [Reuters global markets](https://www.lse.co.uk/news/global-markets-bonds-stocks-selloff-pauses-as-oil-retreats-from-multi-month-high-d6ks9tlw6ltbggb.html))

Breadth confirms that this was not simply a large-cap index wobble. Only three of 11 sector ETFs rose; energy led, while health care and materials were the largest laggards. Growth outperformed value, small caps materially underperformed, HYG and LQD price proxies weakened, and Bitcoin fell. The weekly regime was therefore **selectively defensive and inflation-sensitive**, even though VIX never closed at a disorderly level and large-cap technology held up comparatively well.

## Global week

The tightening was global. Reuters reported the STOXX Europe 600 down about **2%** for the week as the ECB raised rates and German yields rose; the saved VGK U.S.-traded Europe ETF proxy lost **1.88%**. EEM fell **1.25%** and the China-focused MCHI proxy lost **3.55%**, while EWJ gained **0.29%** despite a 1.9% Friday fall in Japan's Nikkei. Regional ETFs trade in U.S. hours and embed currency effects, so they are comparable investor proxies rather than local-index returns. ([Reuters global markets](https://www.lse.co.uk/news/global-markets-bonds-stocks-selloff-pauses-as-oil-retreats-from-multi-month-high-d6ks9tlw6ltbggb.html); [Associated Press Friday close](https://apnews.com/article/financial-markets-stocks-oil-iran-war-bonds-rates-8c3272812f5e9b9238c6a3301921c17a))

Rates carried the shock across regions. Germany's 10-year yield was up 17 basis points through Friday morning, Japan's 10-year yield approached 3%, and the U.S. 10-year neared 5%. The ECB raised all three policy rates by 25 basis points, taking the deposit facility to 2.50% effective September 16, and explicitly tied the move to Middle East-driven inflation pressure. The cross-market interpretation is that investors demanded more compensation for both inflation and duration at the same time; the nearly flat dollar suggests the repricing was not uniquely American. ([ECB monetary-policy decision](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp260910~314e508016.en.html); [Reuters global bond selloff](https://ca.marketscreener.com/news/global-bond-selloff-pushes-10-year-us-yield-toward-5-on-oil-rate-hike-fears-ce785bdfdb8bf727))

## Macro and earnings pulse

This week's data changed the policy debate from whether inflation was reaccelerating to how much tightening is needed to contain it.

- **Producer inflation:** August final-demand PPI rose **0.4% month over month** and **5.4% year over year**. Final-demand goods increased 1.1%, led by a 4.2% energy rise; diesel jumped 24.1%, while transportation and warehousing services rose 2.3%. ([BLS PPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ppi.nr0.htm))
- **Consumer inflation:** August CPI rose **0.4% month over month** and **3.4% year over year**. Core CPI rose 0.3% in the month but eased to 2.4% year over year. Energy increased 2.1% in August, gasoline rose 3.9%, and energy prices were 16.3% above a year earlier. The composition is inflationary, but the easing in year-over-year core inflation prevents a one-directional conclusion. ([BLS CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm))
- **Policy expectations:** AP reported that Friday's release strengthened expectations for a September Fed hike, and Schwab recorded an 88% market-implied probability after CPI. That is a time-stamped market snapshot, not a forecast or a guarantee of the decision. The 2-year yield's move to 4.64% is the cleaner saved implication: policy expectations tightened materially. ([Associated Press Friday close](https://apnews.com/article/financial-markets-stocks-oil-iran-war-bonds-rates-8c3272812f5e9b9238c6a3301921c17a); [Schwab options update](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/todays-options-market-update))
- **Global policy:** the ECB's 25-basis-point increase and higher 2027–28 inflation projections show that the energy shock is complicating disinflation outside the United States as well. ([ECB decision](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp260910~314e508016.en.html))
- **Earnings and dispersion:** Oracle initially surged after stronger-than-expected results but reversed during Friday's session; Kroger gained after beating profit expectations while trimming an underlying sales-growth forecast. Those moves support company-specific dispersion rather than a broad earnings conclusion. ([Associated Press Friday close](https://apnews.com/article/financial-markets-stocks-oil-iran-war-bonds-rates-8c3272812f5e9b9238c6a3301921c17a))

The combined inference is uncomfortable but not recessionary: inflation pressure broadened through energy and freight while equities, credit proxies and duration weakened. At the same time, core CPI's annual rate eased and large-cap technology remained resilient. The market is pricing a tighter policy path, but the evidence does not establish that growth has already rolled over.
