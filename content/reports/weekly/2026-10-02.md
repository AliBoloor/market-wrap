## Week in review

The week delivered a clear split: **large-cap technology held up while long-duration financial conditions tightened and broad participation weakened**. The S&P 500 cash index slipped **0.27%**, the Dow lost **1.26%**, and the Russell 2000 fell **0.16%**, while the Nasdaq Composite gained **0.45%**. Technology was the strongest sector proxy, growth beat value by 1.87 percentage points, and only three of 11 sector ETFs advanced. At the same time, the official 10-year Treasury yield rose **11 basis points to 5.28%**, the curve bear-steepened, TLT lost **2.32%**, and both investment-grade and high-yield bond ETF prices declined.

Friday softened—but did not reverse—that regime. September payrolls rose only **29,000**, the prior two months were revised down by a combined 60,000, and wage growth slowed to 0.1% month over month. The report cut the market-implied probability of an October Fed hike to below 23% from 64% a week earlier, according to AP. Equities rallied and VIX fell, but the official 10-year yield still closed at 5.28%, above the prior Friday's 5.17%. ([BLS employment report](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm); [AP Friday close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-bonds-us-oil-a2b99562febc21d84955e243c87f5d31))

The cross-asset message was therefore **mixed risk appetite with a restrictive rates core**. The dollar rose, gold fell, credit proxies weakened, and European and China-focused equity proxies declined. VIX rose only 0.44 point on the week and finished at 15.31, so the evidence does not show acute equity stress. It does show that AI-linked earnings optimism and reduced near-term Fed-hike odds were offsetting—rather than eliminating—pressure from inflation, energy, fiscal and term-premium risks. The relative weight of those bond-market drivers remains inconclusive.

## Global week

The saved U.S.-traded regional proxies showed a wide dispersion: Japan ETF EWJ gained **1.01%**, while Europe ETF VGK fell **2.54%**, emerging-markets ETF EEM lost **0.46%**, and China-focused MCHI declined **2.62%**. These dollar-denominated ETFs include currency and U.S.-hours effects and are not local cash-index returns.

The global bond shock was more uniform than the equity outcome. On Thursday, AP reported losses of 1.7% in London, 1.6% in Paris and 1.0% in Frankfurt as sovereign yields swung sharply; the U.S. 10-year briefly reached about 5.34%, its highest since 2002. Asian trading was also pressured by bond and currency volatility before the U.S. jobs report. ([AP Thursday close](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-inflation-oil-war-cc56b71699c74950fb1bd9564bee74cc); [Reuters global markets](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/asian-shares-fall-after-wild-swings-in-bonds-fx-us-jobs-data-looms-ce785ddadc8cf627))

Energy and Middle East developments remained an important transmission channel. WTI's weekly decline masked a rebound Thursday as uncertainty persisted over restored supply flows and the Strait of Hormuz. China was closed for part of Golden Week, limiting local price discovery. The evidence supports a global sensitivity to duration, energy and currency moves; it does not support treating the week as a synchronized equity selloff.

## Macro and earnings pulse

- **Growth was revised to a still-solid pace.** Real second-quarter GDP grew at a 2.2% annual rate in the third estimate, with consumer spending, investment and exports contributing. That was slower than the revised 2.5% first-quarter pace. ([BEA GDP](https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gross-domestic-product))
- **Spending remained firm while inflation stayed above target.** August nominal consumption rose 0.9% and real consumption 0.6%. Headline PCE prices rose 0.3% on the month and 3.4% over 12 months; core PCE rose 0.2% and 3.0%. The saving rate was 4.1%. ([BEA personal income and outlays](https://bea.gov/index.php/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-august-2026))
- **Labor demand cooled without collapsing.** August job openings were little changed at 7.1 million, with hires at 5.2 million and quits at 3.1 million. September payroll growth then slowed to 29,000; unemployment held at 4.2%, participation at 61.8%, and average hourly earnings rose 3.0% from a year earlier. ([BLS JOLTS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.htm); [BLS employment report](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm))
- **Factory activity and input costs stayed firm.** ISM manufacturing registered 54.5, new orders 55.3 and employment 52.7, while the prices index jumped to 77.9 from 71.1. That combination reinforced the tension between continued activity and inflation pressure. ([ISM manufacturing](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-pmi-reports/pmi/september/))
- **AI investment remained the earnings exception.** Micron reported record fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $54.23 billion and stronger fiscal-2027 expectations, supporting technology shares. Nike's weaker outlook weighed on the consumer-discretionary read. These company results do not establish an economy-wide earnings trend. ([Micron results](https://investors.micron.com/news/press-release/2026/Micron-Technology-Inc--Reports-Record-Fiscal-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2026-Results/default.aspx); [AP Friday close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-bonds-us-oil-a2b99562febc21d84955e243c87f5d31))

Taken together, the data describe **moderate growth, resilient consumption, cooling labor demand and persistent inflation pressure**. Friday's jobs report improved the case for Fed patience, but the PCE and ISM readings explain why the long end did not produce a lasting rally.
