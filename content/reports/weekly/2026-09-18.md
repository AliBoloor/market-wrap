## Week in review

The week ended with **global policy tightening, a 5% U.S. 10-year yield and narrow equity resilience rather than broad risk-on participation**. The S&P 500 cash index slipped **0.08%**, the Dow fell **1.69%**, and the Russell 2000 lost **1.50%**, while the Nasdaq Composite gained **0.72%**. Friday's 0.2% S&P 500 rise left the benchmark nearly unchanged for the week, but most U.S. stocks fell that day. Technology and health care were the only sector ETFs in the saved scorecard to gain for the week; financials, utilities, real estate and materials lagged. ([Associated Press closing record](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-da0dbe004b6f83c36e7d1626a9741a92))

The Federal Reserve raised its target range 25 basis points to **3.75%–4.00%**, its first increase in three years, and its September projections put the median year-end policy rate at 4.1%, consistent with another quarter-point move if the economy evolves as participants expect. The Bank of Japan raised its rate to **1.25%**, the highest in 31 years. The Bank of England held at **3.75%**, but three of nine members voted to hike. Those decisions shared an inflation-risk message even as the U.S. data mix remained uneven. ([Federal Reserve statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm); [Fed projections](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260916.htm); [Bank of England decision](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/september-2026); [Bank of Japan statements](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/state_2026/index.htm))

This is best described as a **mixed but tightening-sensitive regime**. The dollar rose 1.10%, the 2s10s curve flattened through Thursday, regional equity proxies weakened, and small caps/value lagged growth. Yet VIX fell 1.03 points to 14.81, investment-grade bond ETF LQD and long-Treasury ETF TLT rose modestly, gold was nearly unchanged, and Bitcoin gained. The cross-asset evidence therefore shows tighter global policy and weak breadth, not disorderly deleveraging. Oil remained the main inflation risk, but no saved WTI return is reported because Yahoo's September 18 continuous-futures bar failed the repository's OHLC integrity check.

## Global week

The saved U.S.-traded regional proxies were weak: Europe ETF VGK lost **2.01%**, Japan ETF EWJ fell **1.58%**, and emerging-markets ETF EEM declined **1.19%**; China-focused MCHI edged up **0.21%**. These are investable U.S.-hours proxies that include currency effects, not local cash-index returns.

Reuters described a week dominated by central banks responding to energy-driven inflation risk. The Fed hiked, the BOJ raised its rate to 1.25%, and the BOE's hold included three votes for an increase. The dollar strengthened, the yen weakened after the BOJ decision, and sovereign yields remained elevated across major markets. Brent finished Friday at **$103.87 per barrel**, down 0.9% on the day after briefly falling below $102; Reuters reported it was on course for a weekly decline. ([Reuters global markets](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/global-shares-fall-treasury-yields-rise-as-central-banks-double-down-on-inflation-fight-ce785adadc8af724); [Associated Press Friday market report](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-oil-war-inflation-rates-1ff3311788bcc4555d00e283a57289fe))

The interpretation is a synchronized inflation response rather than a synchronized growth boom. Europe and Japan weakened in dollar-based proxy terms even as the Nasdaq gained. The stronger dollar and flatter U.S. curve reinforce the tightening signal, while contained credit proxies and falling VIX argue that markets have not shifted into acute systemic stress.

## Macro and earnings pulse

- **Federal Reserve:** the FOMC voted 12–0 to raise the target range to 3.75%–4.00%, saying activity was expanding at a solid pace, domestic spending was resilient and inflation remained elevated. September medians projected 2026 GDP growth of 2.3%, unemployment of 4.1%, PCE inflation of 3.7%, core PCE inflation of 3.4%, and a 4.1% year-end policy rate. Relative to June, the rate and inflation paths moved higher while growth and employment projections improved. ([Fed statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm); [Fed projections](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260916.htm))
- **Consumer demand:** August retail and food-services sales rose **1.2% month over month** and 6.0% year over year, but the figures are nominal and therefore include price effects. July was revised to -0.5%. ([Census retail sales](https://www.census.gov/retail/sales.html))
- **Housing:** total August starts fell **2.6%** to a 1.275 million annual rate and permits fell 2.7%. Single-family starts rose 7.6%, but that estimate carried a ±14.0% margin, so the apparent rebound is not statistically decisive. ([Census housing construction](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/current/))
- **Production:** August industrial production was unchanged; manufacturing output fell 0.3%, mining rose 0.1%, and utilities gained 1.8%. Total production was 1.4% above a year earlier. ([Federal Reserve industrial production](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/current/default.htm))
- **Global policy:** the BOE held but said inflation risks had tilted further upward, while the BOJ's increase extended tightening beyond the United States and euro area. The result is a higher global discount-rate floor while the Middle East energy shock remains unresolved. ([BOE decision](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/september-2026); [BOJ statements](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/state_2026/index.htm))
- **Corporate dispersion:** AP reported Nucor fell 6.3% after third-quarter guidance missed expectations despite improved steel-mill profit, while Coinbase and Robinhood rallied. These company-specific moves do not support a broad earnings conclusion, but they show how financing conditions, commodity costs and idiosyncratic news are separating winners from laggards. ([AP Friday report](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-oil-war-inflation-rates-1ff3311788bcc4555d00e283a57289fe))

The evidence is consistent with **resilient nominal demand but a less comfortable inflation-growth mix**: spending is firm, the Fed raised its growth forecast, and credit proxies are stable, while production is flat, housing is mixed and energy remains expensive. Calling this stagflation would be premature; calling it a benign disinflation regime would ignore the policy and yield repricing.

## Data quality and limitations

- The deterministic collector saved 32 fresh market histories with September 18 observations and no synthetic substitutions.
- WTI continuous futures were excluded because the September 18 Yahoo bar reported an open above its high, violating the repository's OHLC integrity rule. The failure is retained in `failures.json`; oil discussion instead uses separately cited Brent observations. No weekly WTI return is estimated.
- Weekly changes are unadjusted price returns. Cash indexes, ETF proxies, continuous futures, yields and spot assets are identified separately.
- Yahoo daily timestamps differ by venue. U.S. equity bars represent the September 18 session; continuous futures and Bitcoin are not simultaneous with the U.S. equity close.
- The official 2-year yield and 2s10s curve are through September 17. The Friday 10-year observation is separately time-labeled rather than silently combined into a Friday curve.
- HYG and LQD are ETF price proxies, not credit-spread series. Regional ETFs include currency and U.S.-hours effects.
- Options volume and open interest do not identify trade direction or dealer positioning.
- Economic releases are preliminary or revisable where the source says so. Calendar and earnings dates can change.
