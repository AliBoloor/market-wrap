## Week in review

The headline indexes finished almost unchanged, but the cross-asset message was less comfortable than the index returns imply. The S&P 500 gained **0.09%**, the Nasdaq Composite gained **0.40%**, the Russell 2000 added **0.11%**, and the Dow lost **0.27%** from the prior Friday close. Beneath that surface, only four of 11 sector ETFs rose, value beat growth, high-yield and investment-grade bond ETFs fell, and the 10-year Treasury yield rose **6.4 basis points** to **4.784%**. VIX nevertheless closed at only **14.53**. The regime was therefore **resilient but narrow and inflation-sensitive**, not broadly risk-on.

The week's turning points were unusually clear. Renewed U.S.–Iran strikes lifted oil and yields and pressured equities early in the week. On Thursday, Governor Christopher Waller said continued disinflation could justify holding the policy rate steady, supporting a bond-and-equity relief rally. Friday's **162,000** payroll gain, unchanged **4.1%** unemployment rate, and upward revisions to June and July then restored the case for a September hike; stocks gave back part of Thursday's advance and Treasury yields rose. These attributions are supported by official releases and contemporaneous reporting, although they do not explain every company or sector move. ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20260903a.htm); [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_09042026.htm); [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-oil-trump-iran-war-1af16359af43eb8abc66445465f633c8))

Oil was the macro transmission channel that mattered most. The saved continuous WTI futures series gained **9.38%** to **$91.22**, while Reuters reported the front WTI contract settled Friday at **$91.48** and gained nearly 10% for the week. That energy shock, combined with firm activity data, raises the hurdle for benign inflation prints even though the dollar index fell over the full week. ([Reuters oil close](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/oil-set-for-steepest-weekly-gain-since-mid-july-fuelled-by-us-iran-clashes-ce785bdadc8af027))

## Macro and earnings pulse

The week's official data describe expansion with persistent price pressure—not recession, and not clean disinflation.

- **Labor demand:** July job openings were little changed at **7.3 million**, while hires and total separations were each little changed at **5.1 million**. Friday's payroll report then showed August employment up 162,000, unemployment steady at 4.1%, and prior payrolls revised higher by 55,000 in total. ([BLS JOLTS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/jolts_09012026.htm); [BLS employment](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_09042026.htm))
- **Manufacturing:** August ISM manufacturing eased to **54.6** from 55.6 but remained expansionary for an eighth month. New orders slowed to 53.7 and the prices index stayed high at **71.1**. ([ISM manufacturing](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-pmi-reports/pmi/august/))
- **Services:** August ISM services rose to **55.4** from 54.1, with strong business activity and new orders but the highest prices reading in nearly four years. ([ISM services](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/news-publications/inside-supply-management-magazine/blog/2026/2026-09/ism-pmi-reports-roundup-august-2026-services/))
- **Policy:** Waller made the August inflation data the decisive near-term input: continued progress would support a hold, while renewed inflation could justify a hike at the September 15–16 FOMC meeting. Friday's labor report strengthened the hike side of that conditional distribution, but it did not settle the decision. ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20260903a.htm))
- **Earnings and dispersion:** Lululemon fell 17.4% Friday after missing revenue expectations and cutting its full-year outlook, while several memory-chip stocks advanced. The contrast supports a company-specific consumer-versus-AI dispersion story; it does not establish a broad earnings recession. ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-oil-trump-iran-war-1af16359af43eb8abc66445465f633c8))

## Data quality and limitations

- All saved price histories are clipped at September 4; no later observation enters a calculation or chart. The latest U.S. session observation is the September 4 close.
- Yahoo Finance close-to-close moves are price returns, not dividend-adjusted total returns. ETF proxies are labeled and are not blurred with cash indexes, local indexes, futures, yields or credit spreads.
