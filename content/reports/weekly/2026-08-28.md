## Week in review

The week ended with a modest gain for large U.S. equities but a meaningful split beneath the headline. The S&P 500 and Dow each gained **0.5%**, the Nasdaq Composite gained **0.8%**, and the Russell 2000 lost **1.5%**. That combination reads as cautiously risk-on at the index level but narrow and rate-sensitive underneath: megacap growth held up while smaller companies did not. ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-3f3477bcea915ac53ec2ae905ae57919))

The week's central tension was whether softer activity and lower oil would buy the Federal Reserve time, or whether inflation persistence would force another increase. Falling oil and yields supported equities early in the week, while Nvidia's results confirmed that AI infrastructure spending remained powerful. By Friday, Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech shifted attention back to inflation control. Rate-hike probability for September rose to 55.7% from 35.4% the prior day; the two-year yield jumped almost 13 basis points Friday and small caps lagged. ([Reuters cross-asset close](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/stocks-fall-while-dollar-bond-yields-rise-as-warsh-prompts-rate-hike-bets-ce7858dfd080f724); [Federal Reserve speech](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/warsh20260828a.htm))

The resulting regime was **mixed rather than cleanly risk-on**. SPY finished almost exactly on its 20-day average and above its rising 50- and 200-day averages, but IWM ended below both its 20- and 50-day averages. VIX fell for the week, yet Friday's front-end yield shock and the stronger dollar warned that equity duration remained exposed to inflation surprises.

## Macro and earnings pulse

The official data said demand remained present but inflation was not yet defeated. Second-quarter U.S. GDP was confirmed at a **1.5% annualized** rate, but private domestic final sales grew 4.2%. July real consumer spending was essentially flat, while headline and core PCE prices both rose 0.2% in the month; headline PCE inflation was 3.7% year over year. The mix supports neither an imminent recession nor an easy return to 2% inflation. ([BEA GDP](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-second-estimate-and-corporate-profits-2nd-quarter-2026); [BEA personal income and outlays](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-july-2026))

At the broader index level, FactSet's August 7 snapshot put blended second-quarter S&P 500 earnings growth at 50.4%, but only 32.0% excluding Alphabet and Amazon—excellent aggregate results with meaningful concentration and accounting caveats.
