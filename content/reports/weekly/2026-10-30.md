## Week in review

The week was a **rotation away from mega-cap growth, not a broad credit or small-cap unwind**. The S&P 500 cash index lost **0.57%**, the Nasdaq Composite fell **2.00%**, and the Dow declined **0.85%**, while the Russell 2000 gained **1.29%**. Value ETF VTV advanced 1.32% as growth ETF VUG fell 1.96%. Nine of the eleven tracked sector ETFs rose: health care gained 3.12% and materials 2.79%, while communication services fell 5.32% and technology lost 2.03%.

The path was more fragile than the weekly S&P 500 number suggests. Monday through Wednesday produced a 1.58% SPX rebound, helped first by technology and then by the Federal Reserve holding its target range at 3.75%–4.00%. Thursday's Alphabet-led decline still came with positive rotation beneath the capitalization-weighted indexes. Friday broadened the damage as Intel guidance and Amazon's spending and cash-flow outlook intensified concern about returns on AI investment; the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.9% that day and SPX posted its worst session since June. The evidence supports **concentrated growth de-rating with defensive and small-cap participation**, followed by a material Friday risk-off close—not an economy-wide liquidation. ([Monday close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-oil-earnings-trump-be3a56f58a77c5be863f0289d280be92); [Tuesday close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-ai-earnings-oil-e11a2240fc3283c4507d44b9833f30a2); [Thursday close](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-2c26b0b1bbc3bfab9f3f4b902172f458); [Friday close](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-ai-alphabet-oil-16c266731fdf64178f38070af2b42497))

Cross-asset signals were defensive but not disorderly. Official two- and ten-year Treasury par yields fell 13 and 6 basis points, respectively, and TLT gained 0.61%. HYG and LQD price proxies both rose. VIX ended almost unchanged week over week at 19.42 only because it started from an already elevated 19.45; it traded as high as 23.93 Friday. Gold and Bitcoin weakened sharply, while oil was nearly flat. Falling yields and firm credit proxies limited the systemic-risk message, but the yield-curve steepening, high volatility and technology break left the equity regime fragile.

## Macro and earnings pulse

- **The Fed held policy restrictive and pushed against an easy-growth interpretation.** The target range stayed at 3.75%–4.00%. The statement described activity as strong, spending as resilient and inflation as elevated; two voters preferred a quarter-point increase. Equities initially absorbed the decision, but the 10-year par yield reached 5.05% Wednesday before falling to 4.94% Friday. ([Federal Reserve statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm))
- **Growth accelerated while inflation remained above comfort.** BEA estimated third-quarter real GDP growth at 3.4% annualized and private domestic final sales at 4.9%. September headline PCE inflation was 3.6% year over year and core PCE 3.1%. That mix helps explain why policy expectations remained sensitive even as yields fell late in the week. ([GDP advance estimate](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-3rd-quarter-2026); [personal income and outlays](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-september-2026))

- **Earnings separated underlying demand from capital-intensity risk.** Early-week AI strength lifted the indexes despite weak breadth, but Alphabet's record Thursday decline and Friday losses in Intel, Amazon and Nvidia turned attention to guidance, capital spending and cash-flow returns. The market reaction—not a generalized revenue collapse—drove the sharp style divergence. ([Tuesday earnings context](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-ai-earnings-oil-e11a2240fc3283c4507d44b9833f30a2); [Thursday earnings context](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-2c26b0b1bbc3bfab9f3f4b902172f458); [Friday earnings context](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-ai-alphabet-oil-16c266731fdf64178f38070af2b42497))
