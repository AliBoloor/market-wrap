## Week in review

The week was a **rotation away from growth concentration rather than a uniform equity retreat**. The S&P 500 cash index lost **0.47%** and the Nasdaq Composite fell **0.71%**, their second consecutive weekly declines, while the Dow gained **0.41%** and the Russell 2000 rose **2.00%**. Value ETF VTV advanced 0.73% as growth ETF VUG fell 1.33%. Eight of the eleven tracked sector ETFs gained, but communication services dropped 4.36% and industrials lost 1.87%. That combination explains why capitalization-weighted indexes weakened even as smaller companies and much of the sector map improved.

The path through the week mattered. Monday's rebound was narrow and AI-led; Tuesday broadened into the Dow and small caps; Wednesday again rewarded AI-linked shares. Thursday's selloff was concentrated in large technology and communication names despite positive S&P 500 constituent breadth. Friday then broadened into a three-quarters-declining session as oil rose above recent highs. The evidence therefore supports a market regime of **fragile index concentration, selective breadth, and rising energy sensitivity**, not a simple economy-wide liquidation. ([Monday close](https://apnews.com/article/494fbe4361a0360b2f6368e7a6d8f058); [Tuesday close](https://apnews.com/article/b972b64127db5412a2b02c35ecf95c88); [Wednesday close](https://apnews.com/article/4fa93685cd49e51e7ff0d840fdd38b0d); [Thursday close](https://apnews.com/article/33767691a4a0730b96d6f0c5100ea9b2); [Friday close](https://apnews.com/article/7b47ef3b037bfba4e34f685329b9b963); [Friday index scorecard](https://apnews.com/article/e07c72bc5cbe5dffe352ddf30054e35f))

Cross-asset signals were defensive but not disorderly. Official two- and ten-year Treasury par yields fell 5 and 9 basis points, respectively, and TLT gained 1.61%. HYG and LQD price proxies both gained, with investment grade outperforming. VIX nevertheless finished at 19.45—above its 20-, 50- and 200-day averages—while WTI rose 1.81% and gold fell 4.09%. Falling yields and firm credit proxies limited the risk-off message; high volatility, weak growth leadership and rising oil kept inflation and valuation risks prominent.

## Macro and earnings pulse

- **Housing weakened under restrictive financing conditions.** September housing starts fell 5.2% to a 1.221 million annual rate and permits slipped 0.2% to 1.400 million. Census cautioned that the starts change was not statistically distinguishable from zero because of the survey's confidence interval. Pending home sales fell 7.3% from August and 11.3% from a year earlier across all four regions. ([Census housing](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/current/); [NAR pending sales](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/housing-statistics/pending-home-sales))
- **Labor data did not signal broad contraction.** Initial claims fell to 194,000, a three-month low. That resilience, alongside a still-high two-year yield, leaves the Fed with little confirmation of a rapid labor-market break. ([AP jobless-claims report](https://apnews.com/article/8e962dcdefefa86ebc91ee9c663f1f3b))
- **Consumers remained deeply cautious about prices.** Final October Michigan sentiment was 48.0, nearly unchanged from September's 48.1 and above the preliminary 46.3. Year-ahead inflation expectations were 4.7% and long-run expectations 3.5%. The final survey improves the preliminary headline but does not remove the cost-of-living concern. ([University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers](https://data.sca.isr.umich.edu/))
- **Earnings showed healthy revenue in places but sharpened the AI-investment debate.** Meta reported 26% revenue growth, but quarterly capital expenditure reached $34.91 billion and free cash flow was negative $2.12 billion; the company narrowed full-year capital spending to $135–145 billion. That helped turn strong core-business growth into a financing and return-on-investment question. ([Meta third-quarter results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx))
- **Execution outside mega-cap technology was mixed rather than recessionary.** Netflix reported 12% revenue growth and a 34.6% operating margin; IBM reported 5% revenue growth and raised its full-year free-cash-flow outlook; GM reported $50.0 billion of revenue and $4.1 billion of adjusted EBIT but narrowed its full-year range. Share-price reactions depended heavily on forward guidance, not simply whether quarterly profit beat expectations. ([Netflix shareholder letter](https://ir.netflix.net/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/FINAL-Q3-26-Shareholder-Letter.pdf); [IBM results](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-10-21-IBM-RELEASES-THIRD-QUARTER-RESULTS); [GM results](https://investor.gm.com/news-releases/news-release-details/gm-releases-2026-third-quarter-results-and-updates-full-year))
