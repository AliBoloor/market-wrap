## Week in review

The week was a **volatility-regime break led by technology and energy, not a uniform failure of credit or every equity sector**. The S&P 500 cash index lost **2.68%**, the Nasdaq Composite fell **3.37%**, and the Russell 2000 declined **2.12%**. Growth ETF VUG lost 3.39% versus a 2.20% decline for value ETF VTV. VIX rose 6.20 points to 25.62. The decisive move came Thursday, when SPX fell 4.36%, QQQ lost 6.49%, every tracked sector declined and volatility surged after Wednesday's post-election rally.

The week's path mattered. Monday's weak technology tape coexisted with positive breadth; Tuesday produced a mixed chip-led rebound; Wednesday's split-government relief rally lifted SPX 1.58% and crushed VIX; Thursday reversed that entire move as worries about AI data-center returns, financing and policy exposure broadened into a market-wide liquidation. Friday stabilized SPX by just 0.31% and lifted QQQ 1.40%, but the Dow fell 0.46% and more S&P 500 constituents declined than advanced. That sequence supports a conclusion of **concentrated AI de-rating that became a broad volatility shock**, followed by only partial stabilization—not a repaired risk-on trend. ([Monday close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-ai-earnings-oil-iran-048328e49e47ce7afe6c1fb9d9acf13d); [Tuesday close](https://apnews.com/article/0d235af127a92c68da0cc2c45c028cef); [Wednesday close](https://apnews.com/article/4d12764c0b3ce38ffde3077712811182); [Thursday close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-ai-midterm-oil-trump-7ee9c212805aa5e70cf34d25aab7a36e); [Friday context](https://apnews.com/article/markets-japan-nikkei-oil-ai-bba6f0bf9035a6e6b1c4fef3e8230b89))

Cross-asset confirmation was defensive but stopped short of a systemic credit break. Official two- and ten-year Treasury par yields fell 5 and 8 basis points, respectively; TLT was nearly flat. LQD gained 0.25%, while HYG slipped 0.25%. Gold rose 4.49%, Bitcoin fell 1.83%, and WTI crude collapsed 12.99%. Falling yields, gold strength and higher volatility confirmed risk aversion, but the resilience of investment-grade bonds and the small high-yield proxy decline argue against describing the week as an economy-wide funding seizure.
