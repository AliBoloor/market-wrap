## Week in review

The week was a **technology-led de-risking and value rotation, not an indiscriminate equity liquidation**. The S&P 500 cash index lost **1.95%**, the Nasdaq Composite fell **4.48%**, and the Russell 2000 dropped **3.05%**, while the Dow gained **0.67%**. Value ETF VTV rose 1.28% as growth ETF VUG fell 4.39%. Technology ETF XLK lost 7.70%, but energy, staples, health care and materials gained between 3.01% and 5.02%. On Friday, AP found that slightly more than half of S&P 500 constituents declined while 68 of 75 technology stocks fell—evidence of severe concentration rather than equal damage across the index. ([AP closing report](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-us-ai-oil-tech-warsh-d2655f64db7ec8ae450525d58f97b628); [AP weekly index scorecard](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-c27809783ae3230b44c26e5ee4bea056))

Inflation was the week's macro shock, but bonds did not finish with a simple inflation selloff. Headline CPI rose 0.6% in September and 3.7% over 12 months; final-demand PPI rose 1.3% and 6.3%. Even so, the official two-year Treasury yield fell 6 basis points over the week to 4.74% and the 10-year fell 15 basis points to 5.09%; the 2s10s curve narrowed 9 basis points to +35 basis points. TLT gained 2.66%. This reversal may reflect defensive demand, positioning and confidence that policy will respond, but the evidence does not isolate one cause. ([BLS CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm); [BLS PPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ppi.nr0.htm); [Treasury daily par yields](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value=2026&field_tdr_date_value_month=202610&type=daily_treasury_yield_curve))

The cross-asset mix ended **defensive, but not disorderly**. VIX rose 5.15 points to 19.99; HYG lost 0.48% while LQD gained 0.97%; WTI crude futures jumped 7.71%; and the dollar slipped 0.42%. Oil and trade-price pressure keep the inflation channel open, while higher volatility and weaker lower-quality credit proxies show that risk appetite deteriorated. Falling Treasury yields and gains in value and defensive sectors prevented that deterioration from becoming a uniform flight from all assets.

## Global week

Global risk performance diverged. U.S.-traded ETF proxies showed Europe (VGK) up 1.47%, Japan (EWJ) down 3.05%, China equities (MCHI) down 2.57%, and emerging markets (EEM) down 5.91%. These are dollar-denominated proxies affected by currencies and U.S. trading hours, not local cash-index returns. AP reported European and Asian markets higher on Friday even as U.S. technology sold off, so Friday was not a synchronized global equity shock.

Oil was the main global transmission channel. AP reported Brent at $109.86 on Friday, up sharply from before the Iran war, with supply still constrained in the absence of a U.S.–Iran agreement. That backdrop helps explain energy-sector resilience and raises risks to household purchasing power, margins and central-bank inflation progress; it does not by itself explain every equity move. ([AP closing report](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-us-ai-oil-tech-warsh-d2655f64db7ec8ae450525d58f97b628))

## Macro and earnings pulse

- **Consumer inflation reaccelerated:** September CPI rose 0.6% month over month and 3.7% year over year. Gasoline rose 9.1%, but shelter rose 0.4%; core CPI increased 0.3% and 2.6% over 12 months. ([BLS CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm))
- **Pipeline inflation was broader and hotter:** final-demand PPI rose 1.3% for the month and 6.3% over 12 months. The measure excluding food, energy and trade services rose 0.5% and 5.0%, respectively. ([BLS PPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ppi.nr0.htm))
- **Nominal demand stayed firm:** September retail and food-services sales rose 0.8% from August and 6.3% from a year earlier. The data are not adjusted for price changes, so they do not establish the same increase in real consumption. ([Census retail sales](https://www.census.gov/retail/sales.html))
- **Imported inflation intensified while production held up:** import prices rose 1.1% in September and 8.1% over 12 months; export prices rose 2.2% for the month. Industrial production increased 0.7%, manufacturing output rose 0.3%, and third-quarter production advanced at a 2.8% annualized rate. ([BLS import/export prices](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ximpim_10162026.htm); [Federal Reserve industrial production](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/current/default.htm))
- **Earnings did not validate one uniform slowdown:** financial-sector ETF XLF gained 1.44% even as individual bank results were mixed. TSMC reported third-quarter revenue 50.9% above a year earlier, yet its U.S. shares sold off after results as the market questioned the financing and durability of AI investment. The result supports ongoing semiconductor demand; the price response shows that strong growth alone was insufficient at prevailing expectations and discount rates. ([TSMC third-quarter management report](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-10/f83780000ddc0c3b48e57a87eab6814c4ab35058/3Q26%20ManagementReport.pdf); [AP closing report](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-us-ai-oil-tech-warsh-d2655f64db7ec8ae450525d58f97b628))
