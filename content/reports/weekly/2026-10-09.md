## Week in review

The week produced a **record-setting large-cap advance with rotation beneath the surface, not a uniform risk-on move**. The S&P 500 cash index gained **1.15%**, the Dow rose **0.93%**, and the Nasdaq Composite added **0.64%**, while the Russell 2000 fell **0.91%**. Nine of 11 sector ETFs advanced and value beat growth, but small caps remained weak. Energy, utilities, staples and health care led; technology and industrials were the only sector proxies to decline. ([Associated Press Friday close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-oil-ai-bonds-5d0f953dbf96febb0690c1aef23fa8a9); [Associated Press weekly index table](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-dafbd0c4037ee10e2a9e305f3cfa8e70))

Rates were volatile without finishing higher. The official two-year Treasury yield fell **3 basis points to 4.80%** and the 10-year fell **4 basis points to 5.24%** from the prior Friday; the 2s10s curve narrowed one basis point to **+44 basis points**. The path mattered more than the endpoint: the 10-year reached 5.36% on Wednesday before strong 10- and 30-year auction demand helped it retreat. TLT gained 0.65%, but it remains below falling 20-, 50- and 200-day averages. ([U.S. Treasury daily rates](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value_month=202610&type=daily_treasury_yield_curve); [Associated Press Thursday close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-bonds-oil-us-6a096d714c874db13632794a32cebaa6))

The cross-asset mix is **constructive for large-cap equities but still macro-fragile**. VIX fell 0.47 point to 14.84 and credit ETF prices recovered, while oil, gold and the dollar all rose and Bitcoin declined. The week showed that record equities can coexist with high real-economy borrowing costs, energy-driven inflation risk and unusually elevated rate and credit volatility. That is resilience, not resolution.

## Macro and earnings pulse

- **Services stayed firm while price pressure intensified.** September ISM Services eased to 54.9 from 55.4, but new orders remained strong at 59.8 and the prices index rose to 74.0, its highest since July 2022. That combination reinforced the market's growth-versus-inflation tension. ([ISM Services PMI](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/services-pmi-at-54-9-september-2026-ism-services-pmi-report-302898421.html))
- **Imports widened the trade deficit.** The August goods-and-services deficit rose 13.7% to $105.6 billion; imports increased 4.3% and exports 1.4%. Capital-goods imports rose $6.2 billion, including a $2.4 billion increase in semiconductors. The data point to firm domestic and investment demand, but the GDP contribution depends on inventories and subsequent revisions. ([BEA and Census trade release](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/us-international-trade-goods-and-services-august-2026))
- **The Fed retained an inflation-first bias.** September minutes showed unanimous support for the 25-basis-point hike to 3.75%–4.00%; participants generally saw solid activity, inflation risks tilted upward and labor-market risks broadly balanced. Governor Waller said inflation remained too high and anticipated additional hikes if data evolved as expected, while allowing that they need not occur at consecutive meetings. ([FOMC minutes](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20260916.htm); [Waller speech](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20261008a.htm))
- **Labor layoffs remained low, but consumers became more cautious.** Initial claims fell to 197,000. Preliminary October Michigan sentiment fell to 46.3 from 48.1, while one-year inflation expectations rose to 4.7% and long-run expectations to 3.5%. The survey and strong services prices complicate the disinflation case despite weak September payroll growth. ([University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers](https://www.sca.isr.umich.edu/))
- **Company news widened dispersion.** PepsiCo rose after beating profit and revenue expectations; Delta reported adjusted EPS of $1.72 and strong travel demand but faces a large fuel-cost headwind. Semiconductors sold off Thursday, while Humana and cell-tower owners surged Friday on company-specific developments. These outcomes do not yet establish an economy-wide earnings trend; next week's banks and semiconductor suppliers are the first broader test.
