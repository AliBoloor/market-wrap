## Closing assessment

The strong August employment report undid much of Thursday's dovish repricing without producing a disorderly selloff. Nonfarm payrolls rose **162,000**, the unemployment rate held at **4.1%**, and June and July payrolls were revised up by a combined 55,000. The S&P 500 fell 0.4%, the Dow declined 0.5%, and the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.3% as Treasury yields rose. The two-year yield moved to about 4.38% from 4.34% and the 10-year to 4.78% from 4.77%. ([BLS employment report](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_09042026.htm); [Associated Press close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-oil-trump-iran-war-1af16359af43eb8abc66445465f633c8))

The causal chain is unusually well supported: the payroll surprise strengthened the case for a September rate increase, short-rate expectations rose, and rate-sensitive equities weakened. Reuters reported that rate futures moved to roughly a 65% probability of a hike from about 55% before the release. Yet the tape was **mixed rather than fully risk-off**. QQQ and IWM finished slightly higher even as SPY fell, memory-chip shares rallied, and VIX rose only modestly to 14.53. This suggests the shock was absorbed as a policy repricing, not interpreted as a near-term growth break. ([Reuters rates and markets](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/yields-rise-stocks-mostly-ease-after-solid-u-s-jobs-report-ce785bdad08ff12c); [Reuters equity reaction](https://ca.marketscreener.com/news/wall-st-slides-after-strong-jobs-report-bolsters-hawkish-fed-bets-ce785bdbd98af127))

The report also improved the underlying labor narrative. Food services and drinking places added 59,000 jobs and local government education added 42,000, while information employment fell. Because inflation remains above the Fed's target and oil is near $91, the market's immediate question shifts from whether hiring is deteriorating to whether next Friday's CPI confirms enough inflation pressure to justify a hike. The long Labor Day weekend adds headline and gap risk before Tuesday's reopening.

Company dispersion reinforced that this was not a uniform macro liquidation. Nvidia, AMD, SanDisk and Micron advanced, while Lululemon fell sharply after missing revenue expectations and cutting its full-year outlook. In interpretation, the market rewarded AI and memory exposure but punished company-specific consumer weakness even while the aggregate labor data looked resilient. ([Associated Press close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-oil-trump-iran-war-1af16359af43eb8abc66445465f633c8))
