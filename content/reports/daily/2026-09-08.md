## Closing assessment

Wall Street returned from the Labor Day weekend to an **oil-led, inflation-sensitive decline**. The S&P 500 lost 0.6%, the Dow fell 1.2%, the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.3%, and the Russell 2000 declined 0.5%. Brent briefly approached $100 and settled at $97.92 as renewed fighting in the Iran war constrained supply; the U.S. 10-year yield rose to 4.80%. That combination raised the discount rate and the expected cost pressure facing companies at the same time. ([Associated Press market close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-trump-oil-ai-rates-d3d6157a534584985987f828a940cffa); [Associated Press index closes](https://apnews.com/article/stock-market-dow-nasdaq-cadd309d4fd4933397cd38fe436edb71))

The session was **risk-off, but not a broad liquidation**. VIX closed at 15.72, only 2.7% higher; QQQ fell just 0.08%; and Qualcomm rose 2.9% after announcing an AI-data-center collaboration with Amazon. By contrast, Boston Scientific lost 5.7% after a cyber-related network outage impaired its outlook, and U.S.-listed Novartis shares fell 14.1% after a disappointing clinical update. The dispersion says investors were repricing energy, rates and specific earnings risks rather than abandoning growth exposure indiscriminately. ([Associated Press market close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-trump-oil-ai-rates-d3d6157a534584985987f828a940cffa))

The near-term market question is now whether oil strength passes through to inflation strongly enough to push the Federal Reserve toward a September hike. AP reported about a 60% implied probability of a 25-basis-point increase at the September 16 decision. Thursday's PPI and Friday's CPI are therefore more important than Wednesday's quieter calendar: a hot pair would validate today's higher-yield, lower-equity reaction, while softer readings could unwind part of it. ([Associated Press market close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-trump-oil-ai-rates-d3d6157a534584985987f828a940cffa); [BLS September calendar](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/2026/09_sched_list.htm))
