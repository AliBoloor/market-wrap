## Closing assessment

Thursday was a **risk-on rebound, led by large-cap technology**, as lower oil prices and easing long-term yields relieved two of the pressures that had built ahead of Wednesday's Federal Reserve hike. The S&P 500 rose 1.1%, the Nasdaq Composite 1.7%, the Dow 0.6%, and the Russell 2000 0.6%. The S&P recovered most of its weekly loss, while Nvidia gained 2.6% and AMD 6.3%. ([AP market close](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-fed-oil-26a4da336d561d213a3f28f260f25d2c); [AP cash-index closes](https://apnews.com/article/a8ce06ff4ffcf66d9f28bd3453bdf998))

The cross-asset picture supports that interpretation, but with an important qualification. Brent fell 1% to $104.82 and the 10-year Treasury yield declined to 4.93% from 5.01%, while VIX fell 12.8% to 15.44. Yet oil remains far above early-summer levels, and the Fed has signaled that another hike may be needed. Thursday therefore looks more like relief from an acute oil-and-yield squeeze than a clean removal of the inflation and policy risks. ([AP market close](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-fed-oil-26a4da336d561d213a3f28f260f25d2c); [Federal Reserve statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm))

Breadth was positive at the index level, but leadership was uneven: QQQ gained 1.73% against IWM's 0.53%, and IWM remains below both its 20- and 50-day averages. For Friday, the main test is whether lower yields can persist and whether participation broadens beyond mega-cap growth as industrial-production data and quarterly options expiration arrive.
