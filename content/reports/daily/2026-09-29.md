## Closing assessment

Tuesday finished **mixed but rates-constrained**. The cash S&P 500 fell 0.2%, the Dow lost 0.3%, the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.1%, and the Russell 2000 declined 0.4%. ETF proxies told the same broad story with one nuance: SPY fell 0.18% and IWM 0.36%, while QQQ gained 0.19%. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to roughly 5.25%, TLT lost 0.50%, and the dollar strengthened. VIX was nearly unchanged at 16.04. The combination is not a panic signal; it is a market still absorbing unusually restrictive long-term yields. ([Associated Press closing report](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-oil-bonds-us-269abea6fd8ea7f314a8c34152788e0c); [Associated Press index close table](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-69778ade7a033a10cb44b9078b6bb15d))

Economic releases weakened without producing a sustained bond rally. August job openings fell to 7.08 million, while both layoffs and voluntary quits declined. Conference Board consumer confidence dropped 6.7 points to 81.9, its lowest reading in 12 years. Yet the 10-year yield briefly reached its highest level in roughly 24 years. That divergence leaves Wednesday's inflation data, rather than Tuesday's softer demand signals, as the next important test of the rates regime. ([Associated Press consumer-confidence report](https://apnews.com/article/b9429c6d4f025d00e6283bfde4e0197d))

Oil provided some relief: WTI futures fell 3.80%, and Brent settled 1.7% lower at $96.16. Energy shares weakened, while CarMax rose 6.3% after results exceeded expectations. Global markets were mixed in Europe and mostly lower in Asia. The overall picture is cautious rather than disorderly: breadth remains weak, but index volatility has not confirmed acute stress.
