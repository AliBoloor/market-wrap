## Closing assessment

Monday was a **selective risk-off session**, not a broad liquidation. The S&P 500 fell 0.5%, the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.6%, the Dow declined 0.3%, and the Russell 2000 shed 0.4%. AI-linked stocks led the weakness, while AP reported that more S&P 500 constituents rose than fell. Oil briefly approached $110 and the 10-year Treasury yield crossed 5% intraday before both retreated from their extremes. That combination—negative capitalization-weighted indexes, positive breadth, higher VIX and a late easing in oil and yields—describes concentrated growth-stock pressure inside a more resilient market. ([Associated Press market close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-oil-ai-rates-0b44bfb43960c6ae850567c0c4e5003a); [Associated Press index closes](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-8f72a301be85728018018735163f4dad))

The session had two supported drivers. AP linked the worldwide AI selloff to industry leaders calling for a deliberate slowdown over safety concerns; Nvidia fell 2.6%, SoftBank lost 10.7% in Tokyo, and South Korea's Kospi dropped 3.3%. Separately, Brent settled 1% higher at $105.68 after nearing $110, as an attacked Saudi pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz was expected to remain mostly unavailable for weeks. The 10-year yield briefly reached 5.00% before closing near 4.96%. These channels reinforced each other early, although the later oil retreat and broader equity participation limited the index decline. ([Associated Press market close](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-oil-ai-rates-0b44bfb43960c6ae850567c0c4e5003a))

Market structure nevertheless weakened. SPY held just 0.26% above its 50-day average, QQQ closed 0.16% below its falling 50-day average, and IWM remained 2.76% below its own. VIX rose 8.0% to 17.10 and above-average VIX-option activity showed elevated attention to near-term event risk. Tuesday begins the two-day FOMC meeting, so the next session is likely to be governed by positioning around Wednesday's retail-sales release and rate decision as much as by Monday's headlines.
