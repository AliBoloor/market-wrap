## Closing assessment

Friday finished **risk-on, but with an important rates caveat**. The S&P 500 rose 0.7%, the Dow gained 0.5%, the Nasdaq Composite advanced 1.2%, and the Russell 2000 added 0.9%. The catalyst was a softer September employment report: payrolls increased by only 29,000, unemployment held near 4.2%, wage growth slowed to 0.1% month over month and 3.0% year over year, and July-August payrolls were revised down by a combined 60,000. That combination reduced the market-implied probability of an October Fed hike to less than 23% from 64% a week earlier. ([BLS Employment Situation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm); [Associated Press closing report](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-bonds-us-oil-a2b99562febc21d84955e243c87f5d31))

The rally was not a clean duration-led move. The 10-year yield initially fell below 5.17% but rebounded to about 5.28% as Brent recovered from an intraday drop and settled near $102.25. Stocks consequently gave back part of an earlier 1.2% S&P gain. The signal for Monday is therefore mixed: weaker labor data supported growth stocks and reduced immediate tightening risk, but oil and long-end yields remain high enough to challenge valuations. Nvidia gained 1.3%, Tesla rose 4.7% after stronger deliveries, and Nike fell 3.6% after weak revenue and profit guidance. ([Associated Press closing report](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-bonds-us-oil-a2b99562febc21d84955e243c87f5d31))
