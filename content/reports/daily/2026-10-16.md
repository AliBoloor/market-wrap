## Closing assessment

Friday was a **risk-off, technology-led selloff** that broke important short-term support. The S&P 500 fell 1.1% to 7,659.44, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.1%, the Russell 2000 lost 1.7%, and the Dow declined a comparatively modest 0.3%. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq both recorded their worst week since June; over the week the Nasdaq lost 4.5% while the Dow gained 0.7%. That divergence confirms that the damage remained concentrated in technology and AI-linked shares rather than becoming an equal-weight liquidation. ([Associated Press closing report](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-us-ai-oil-tech-warsh-d2655f64db7ec8ae450525d58f97b628); [AP index scorecard](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-c27809783ae3230b44c26e5ee4bea056))

The day's macro news did not offer an easy inflation-growth trade. Import prices rose 1.1% in September and 8.1% from a year earlier, while export prices rose 2.2%. Industrial production nevertheless increased 0.7%, with manufacturing up 0.3%. The 10-year Treasury yield eased only 1 basis point to 5.09%, oil rose and VIX jumped 21.4% to 19.99 after reaching 22.31 intraday. AP attributed much of the equity pressure to renewed doubts about debt-financed AI spending; the cross-asset evidence supports a defensive regime, though it does not prove one catalyst caused every move. ([BLS import/export prices](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ximpim_10162026.htm); [Federal Reserve industrial production](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/current/default.htm); [Associated Press closing report](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-us-ai-oil-tech-warsh-d2655f64db7ec8ae450525d58f97b628))

## What mattered today

- **Import inflation broadened:** September import prices rose 1.1% month over month and 8.1% year over year. Fuel imports rose 4.8%, but nonfuel imports also increased 0.7% and were 6.0% higher than a year earlier. Export prices rose 2.2% for the month and 11.6% over the year. ([BLS import/export prices](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ximpim_10162026.htm))
- **Production remained resilient:** industrial production rose 0.7% in September and at a 2.8% annualized rate in the third quarter. Manufacturing output rose 0.3%, while capacity utilization reached 76.9%, still 2.5 percentage points below its long-run average. ([Federal Reserve industrial production](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/current/default.htm))
- **AI financing concerns intensified:** AP reported Micron down 5.6%, Intel down 5.8%, and material declines in AMD, Broadcom, Meta and Alphabet as investors questioned the durability of debt-financed AI spending. ([Associated Press closing report](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-us-ai-oil-tech-warsh-d2655f64db7ec8ae450525d58f97b628))
- **Energy resisted the selloff:** Exxon and Chevron advanced as oil stayed elevated amid the Iran conflict and constrained global supply.
- **The weekly split was stark:** Nasdaq fell 4.5% and Russell 2000 fell 3.0%, while the Dow gained 0.7%. That is rotation and concentration risk, not an indiscriminate market collapse. ([AP index scorecard](https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-c27809783ae3230b44c26e5ee4bea056))
