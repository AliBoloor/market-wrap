## Closing assessment

Monday closed **risk-off, with the long end of the Treasury curve doing more damage than oil's final move**. SPY fell 0.74%, QQQ 1.07%, and IWM 0.69%; TLT lost 0.88%, the 10-year yield proxy closed at 5.24%, and VIX rose 8.1% to 16.07. The dollar firmed, gold futures fell 3.82%, and Bitcoin declined 1.19%. Together, those moves point to tighter discount-rate conditions rather than a narrow equity-sector story.

The session began with renewed oil and geopolitical pressure after the United States rejected an Iranian proposal concerning the Strait of Hormuz. Brent briefly traded above $101 but settled only 0.4% higher at $97.83; the 10-year Treasury yield nevertheless rose to 5.24% from 5.17%, near levels last seen in 2007. That distinction matters: oil helped start the move, but high yields persisted after crude pared most of its advance. ([AP report via The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/09/28/stock-markets-oil-war-inflation/f1f2b682-baf2-11f1-81fc-9b76f8343b6c_story.html))

Nvidia gained 2.1% after expanding its buyback authorization, but airlines, gold miners and much of the broader market weakened. SPY remains marginally above its rising 20- and 50-day averages, while IWM is below both. The result is a still-bullish large-cap trend with weak participation and unusually high sensitivity to rates.
