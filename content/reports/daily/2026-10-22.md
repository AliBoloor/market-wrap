## Closing assessment

Thursday was a **large-cap technology selloff rather than a broad liquidation**. The S&P 500 fell 1.2% to 7,663.43 for its worst day since July and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.6%, while the Dow edged up less than 0.1%. Meta lost 7.0%, Nvidia 4.4%, Tesla 3.1%, Lam Research 11.8% and CoreWeave 10.1% as investors reassessed AI valuations, financing costs and capital intensity. Yet two-thirds of S&P 500 constituents rose, IWM finished slightly higher, and Procter & Gamble gained 5.6%. The index-level damage was severe but unusually concentrated. ([Associated Press closing report](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-rates-earnings-oil-33767691a4a0730b96d6f0c5100ea9b2))

Cross-asset confirmation was decisively defensive. VIX jumped 14.1% to 19.03, TLT rallied 1.9%, the 10-year Treasury yield fell to about 4.97% from 5.13%, and Bitcoin lost 3.3%. Oil moved the other way: Brent climbed 4% to $113.17 as the Iran war kept supply risk elevated. That mix—technology weakness, a government-bond bid, higher volatility and higher oil—describes a risk-off shock with an inflationary geopolitical overlay, not a simple growth scare.
