# Source and evidence policy

## Source priority

1. Use primary sources for official data, policy decisions, event times, filings, and company announcements: Federal Reserve, BLS, BEA, U.S. Treasury, SEC, exchanges, and issuer investor-relations sites.
2. Use direct market or exchange sources for instrument definitions and timestamps when available.
3. Use established financial newsrooms for breaking context and reported attribution.
4. Use aggregators only as discovery aids or when the limitation is explicit.

## Evidence rules

- Link directly to the page supporting each material claim.
- Record publication time and observation time when they differ.
- Corroborate causal claims with at least one authoritative attribution or two independent reputable reports.
- Label interpretation explicitly. Do not convert correlation, chronology, or price action alone into causation.
- State when sources conflict, values are delayed, or the evidence is inconclusive.
- Do not cite a search-results page.

## Market observations

- Identify the exact instrument: futures contract, cash index, ETF, yield, currency index, commodity contract, or spot asset.
- Include units, comparison basis, and observation timestamp.
- Keep futures, cash indexes, and ETFs distinct.
- Retain numerical chart inputs and their sources in the publication bundle.

## Public-repository safety

Never publish credentials, tokens, cookies, private messages, local system details, account information, positions, orders, or absolute local paths. Review every generated artifact before commit.
